Investment plan
Step 0 — Credit card never revolvesThe $1,400 balance gets paid to zero every statement, before any investing. Card interest (~22–29% APR if it revolves) beats any market return — in the wrong direction. · If it's already paid in full monthly, you're doing this.
Step 1 — 401k up to the match onlyYour $19k keeps growing. Contribute whatever % your employer matches — that's an instant 50–100% return. Above the match, hold, because of Step 2. · Free money first, always.
Step 2 — Attack the personal loan$24k at $1,200/mo is your biggest monthly line after rent. Every extra dollar here is a guaranteed return at the loan's rate. Any spare cash beyond the match goes here until it's gone. · Guaranteed beats hoped-for.
Step 3 — Emergency fund1 month of expenses minimum while the loan exists, 3–6 months after it's dead. High-yield savings, not invested. · Prevents new debt when life happens.
Step 4 — Then real investingAfter the loan: redirect that $1,200/mo. Max Roth IRA first ($7k/yr) in a broad index fund (VOO / VTI / VT), then raise 401k contributions. · The loan payment becomes your wealth engine.
The Apple $4/dayKeep it if it keeps you in the habit — but it's one company, not a plan. Long-term, index funds should be the base and single stocks the garnish. · ~$122/mo · concentration is the risk, not Apple itself.
Not financial advice — I'm not a financial advisor and I don't know your loan APR or income. The ordering above (match → high-interest debt → emergency fund → index funds) is the standard framework; plug in your actual rates to confirm. If the personal loan is under ~5% APR, investing alongside it becomes defensible.
Yearly items are divided by 12 in the monthly total so an annual charge doesn't hide for 11 months then blow up the 12th. Investments count as cash leaving checking.
